The Tale of Middle-earth

A guide to The Silmarillion, The Hobbit and The Lord of the Rings

First three jewels, then one Ring. Twice a maker pours too much of himself into the work of his hands, and twice the world goes to war over it. Neither treasure is ever won. The jewels end in sky, fire and sea, and the Ring in the fire that made it.

Who's who, in brief
Ilúvatar
God, the One, who makes everything.
The Valar
The great powers who shape and govern the world, called gods by Elves and Men. You will meet Manwë their king, Varda who made the stars, Ulmo of the waters, Yavanna of growing things, Aulë the smith, and Mandos, who keeps the halls of the dead.
The Maiar
Lesser spirits of the same kind. Melian the queen of Doriath, Sauron, the fire-demons called Balrogs, and the wizards, Gandalf among them, are all Maiar.
The Enemy
Melkor, the mightiest of the Valar, who rebels and is later named Morgoth. Sauron is his chief servant, and the enemy after him.
Elves
The Firstborn: deathless, and bound to the world until it ends. Most of this story follows one people of them, the Noldor, master craftsmen whose rebellion drives the First Age.
Men
The Followers, who come later. They die, and at death leave the world for a fate the Elves do not know.
Dwarves and hobbits
Dwarves are made by the smith Aulë. Hobbits, a small, home-loving people, appear only late in the history and carry its ending.

What the books are about

In a letter printed at the front of The Silmarillion, Tolkien says the whole legend is mainly concerned with Fall, Mortality and the Machine. Seven threads to watch for:

Evil cannot make, only mar

Melkor's discord is taken up into the Music and made part of something larger. The wise believe his Orcs were bred from captured Elves, a mockery and not a making. Treebeard says the same in the Third Age: Trolls are counterfeits of Ents, as Orcs are of Elves. Evil never creates anything new, and everything it ruins was good first.

Making turns into owning

Ilúvatar makes and gives away. Fëanor makes and says "mine". Thingol with the Silmaril, Turgon with his city and the Númenóreans with life itself repeat the same fall: love of the work of your own hands above its giver.

Words bind

The Oath cannot be broken or fulfilled, so it drives even Maedhros, who repented, and Maglor into kinslaying again and again. The Doom of Mandos is fulfilled largely through the Noldor's own choices.

A long defeat, with sudden turns

The Elves cannot win and know it. Rescue, when it comes, arrives from outside any reasonable hope: one friend climbing a cliff, a song before a throne, a ship that finally finds the West.

Death is the gift

Elves are bound to the world until it ends and grow weary of it. Men leave it, for somewhere the Elves do not know. In time Men come to envy the Elves their deathlessness and to fear their own gift, and that fear destroys Númenor.

Pity decides the ending

Bilbo spares Gollum under the mountain. Frodo spares him in the hills and again at Faramir's pool, and Sam on the slope of the volcano. No one has the strength to destroy the Ring. It is destroyed because three hobbits each declined to kill.

The small do what the great cannot

The Silmarillion is about the mighty failing: Valar who withdraw, Elven kings who fall, Númenor drowned. Its sequel hands the decisive task to gardeners and burglars whom no power had counted.

The same tale, told twice

The Lord of the Rings keeps rhyming with the earlier ages. Once you see the pairs, the three books lock together.

  • Morgoth, a fallen Vala → Sauron, his lieutenant
  • Three Silmarils, light held in jewels → One Ring, power held in gold
  • Fëanor cannot give up his work → Bilbo leaves the Ring behind, Sam hands it back, Galadriel refuses it
  • Beren and Lúthien → Aragorn and Arwen, their descendants, with Arwen making Lúthien's choice
  • Carcharoth bites off Beren's hand, jewel and all → Gollum bites off Frodo's finger, Ring and all
  • Ungoliant drinks the light of the Trees → Shelob, her last child, is driven off by it
  • Eärendil's Silmaril becomes a star → Galadriel's phial holds that star's light
  • Gondolin falls → its blades, Glamdring and Sting, are carried by Gandalf, Bilbo and Frodo
  • Húrin is shown the world as Morgoth chooses → Denethor sees in his stone only what Sauron chooses
  • The seeing-stones, made by the Noldor in the West → the undoing of Saruman and Denethor
  • Númenor's White Tree is felled and burned → a sapling of its line is found and planted by the returning king

Who reached, who refused

The Ring tests everyone who comes near it. The test is the same one Fëanor failed.

Refused it

  • Gandalf, offered it freely at Bag End
  • Aragorn, who could have taken it on the road and never did
  • Galadriel, offered it in her own garden
  • Faramir, with Frodo at his mercy
  • Sam, who wore it and handed it back

Reached for it

  • Isildur, who would not burn it
  • Sméagol, who killed for it on first sight
  • Saruman, who studied the Rings until he wanted one
  • Boromir, for his city and his own glory in saving it
  • Frodo, at the very end
